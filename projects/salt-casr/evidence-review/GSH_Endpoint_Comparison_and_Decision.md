28 October 2026. Assessment of published evidence and the requirements for a future comparison.

## Answer that the recovered evidence permits

Measured CaSR activity remains a plausible candidate-selection feature, but
this collection cannot establish that it improves selection of human saltiness
enhancers. The new GSH comparison does not provide a usable positive-versus-
negative saltiness experiment: the accessible Goto record establishes mixture
taste intensity, while a separate saltiness outcome remains unverified.
Absence of a recovered endpoint is not proof that it was never measured.

## Relevant additional assay evidence, with a different outcome domain

Kitajima2023 (10.3390/molecules28124585) was recovered as published full XML
and a complete PDF. Figure2C and methods pp9–10 were visually checked. EVG's
reported human-CaSR EC50 is0.167uM in transiently transfected PEAKrapid cells,
with Calcium-5 readout, assay CaCl2 1mM and pH7.4. Keep this as another
assay-specific record, separate from Ohsu's41.9nM; neither average them nor
infer why they differ. Curve SEM is not an EC50 confidence interval, and
independent biological replicate count remains unresolved.

The human test uses IVAH, a non-peptide aldehyde, in0.5%NaCl+0.1%MSG and
asks for a combined intensity choice. It does not supply an EVG sensory
outcome or a separately rated saltiness effect. Nose clips, mock-transfected
cells and inhibitor comparisons are relevant controls, but do not validate a
peptide selector. Figure6B's caption repeats IVAH where the prose describes
IVAA; no numerical comparator label is reconstructed. Ajinomoto provenance
overlaps earlier sources. The preprint is the same study, not another cohort.

Design inference: preserve calcium, pH and assay context rather than attaching
one context-free potency to a compound. A significant unblocked result and a
non-significant blocked result alone do not demonstrate a significant blockade
interaction. Any causal contrast needs its own estimate and uncertainty.

A targeted search for actual functional CaSR plus human saltiness also returned
CN120584963A. Its inspected Examples1–2 concern cat-food intake, hydrolysates
and feline-CaSR activity, not a controlled human saltiness panel. It is excluded
by species, material and outcome, not labeled biologically inactive. The search
also rediscovered Amino2018's already-assessed continuity experiment; it is
not new saltiness evidence. No broader docking screen is justified.

## Written design and execution gate

This refines the existing design; it is not a runnable or registered experiment.

**Hypothesis.** For a prespecified peptide domain, application matrix, sodium
background, exposure rule and saltiness time endpoint, measured functional
human-CaSR information improves useful candidate yield at a fixed follow-up
budget over the same rule without CaSR.

**Competing explanation.** Dose, pH/counterions, sodium, intrinsic flavor,
other taste pathways or mixture interactions account for apparent benefit.
Another possibility is that CaSR predicts combined intensity/continuity but
does not improve selection for the separate saltiness endpoint.

**Eligible actual data.** Require one candidate sampling frame with identified
peptides, compatible functional hCaSR assays and controlled human saltiness
contrasts. Retain all eligible tested candidates, uncertain findings, ranges
and censoring. No present source combination meets this requirement. Goto,
Jung and Kitajima do not increase the current saltiness-condition counts.
Repeated doses of one peptide do not solve the missing candidate denominator.

**Independent unit.** Peptide identity is the selection unit. Family and source
dependence constrain transfer; assessor/session/batch covariance and receptor
biological replicates belong to measurement uncertainty. Shared controls do
not create independent comparisons.

**Primary comparison.** At the same candidate budget, compare a frozen simple
context/chemistry selection rule with that rule plus measured functional CaSR.
Assess peptide-minus-control saltiness under matched conditions. Keep liking,
kokumi, umami, intrinsic taste and mixed intensity as separate outcomes. Keep
multiple receptor assays explicitly separate or select an assay by a rule fixed
before outcomes; do not choose whichever potency gives the preferred result.

**Practical success/failure criterion.** Before confirmation, justify a minimum
useful saltiness effect in the intended application and a minimum incremental
yield of qualifying peptides per budget. Neither is supported by this collection,
so neither is assigned a convenient number. Success requires uncertainty that
resolves useful improvement over baseline. A sufficiently precise gain below
the required minimum supports stopping that selector; wide uncertainty is
inconclusive. A non-significant comparison is not a failure label.

**Uncertainty analysis.** Preserve paired sensory errors, source/family clusters,
receptor assay differences, censoring and selection-related missingness. Report
how coverage of the intended candidate domain limits the inference. Marginal
graph error bars cannot recover paired errors without covariance information.
Do not pool different endpoint scales or infer interactions from P-value changes.

**Decision consequences.** Reliable incremental gain would justify independent
evaluation of that rule in that domain. Informative lack of useful gain would
stop the selector while leaving CaSR involvement in taste open. Missing matched
observations or unset margins stop execution before fitting. Current inspected
evidence remains development material, including every source added here.
