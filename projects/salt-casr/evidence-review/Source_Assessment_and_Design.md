## What remains unproved

The overlap is real at the conventional named-compound level, but scattered
across exposures, salt backgrounds, scales and source histories. A link is not
an experiment demonstrating CaSR-mediated saltiness. Unknown material form,
pH adjustment and sodium can constrain interpretation without proving a
reported sensory difference false. Published significance does not establish
practical usefulness, and non-significance does not establish equivalence.

There is still no single eligible panel with measured functional CaSR and
comparable human saltiness for all its candidates, including unsuccessful and
uncertain conditions. Source selection after successful tasting can bias a
cross-paper collection. Pooling the new observations into a correlation would
make that limitation harder to see.

## Written design before any later test

**Hypothesis:** within a chemically defined peptide domain and a frozen food,
salt background, exposure and taste-time endpoint, measured functional CaSR
information improves useful candidate yield at a fixed sensory follow-up
budget beyond the same selection rule without CaSR.

**Competing explanations:** direct taste, pH/counterion sodium, dose or matrix
interactions may explain perceived saltiness; CaSR may track kokumi rather
than saltiness; different calcium/assay conditions may make a single EC50 an
inadequate feature; publication and prior selection may create apparent gain.

**Eligible actual data:** these records support descriptive source comparisons
and hypothesis refinement. None currently qualifies for the primary selector
comparison. The Givaudan table lacks a matched receptor panel; Wu lacks
functional CaSR; the three named linked peptides lack common conditions and a
sampling frame. No proxy response substitutes for those missing measurements.

**Independent units:** peptide identities are selection units. Related
constitutional families and common experimental sources constrain external
transfer. Assessors, sessions, material batches and receptor biological
replicates define uncertainty, not extra independent candidate identities.

**Primary comparison:** freeze a modest context/chemistry rule, then compare it
with the same rule augmented by actual functional CaSR, using the same eligible
candidates and budget. Measure peptide-minus-background saltiness, preserving
kokumi, umami, intrinsic taste and liking separately. Do not compare model-
predicted potency with manufactured sensory scores. Preserve intervals and
censoring rather than converting ranges to confident point measurements.

**Practical decision criterion:** both the minimum useful sensory change in a
chosen application and the minimum additional useful candidates per follow-up
budget must be justified and frozen before confirmation. No defensible values
are available here, so they remain unset. A favorable point estimate is not
success. An interval excluding the practical minimum would support stopping
the selector; wide uncertainty would remain inconclusive.

**Uncertainty and consequences:** retain paired sensory errors, receptor
replicates, family/source dependence and missingness. Do not infer paired
variance from two unpaired graph error bars. A precise useful gain advances
only the tested prioritization rule and domain; a precise absence of useful
gain changes selection strategy, without refuting CaSR involvement in taste.
Previously inspected evidence remains development material. No experiment,
power calculation or model sweep is justified by current counts alone.
