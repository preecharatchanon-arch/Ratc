# What the recovered original measurements permit

28 October 2026. Read checkpoint version13 before this continuation. No new
scientific numerical test, model fit, docking run or biological/human experiment
was performed. This report records new access to published primary measurements
and a resulting change to evidence qualification.

## Decision

Continue the question of incremental selection value of measured CaSR for
**separately measured human saltiness**. The newly recovered papers strengthen
the receptor and kokumi evidence, but do not provide the common candidate panel
needed to test that selection question. Do not fit their kokumi values as
saltiness labels or pool different assay potencies into a single ground truth.

## 1. Ohsu2010: the outcome is more specific than the abstract suggests

The original article text is now publicly readable via ResearchGate. Its
methods and captions were read, rather than relying on later descriptions.
The actual PDF pages/figure axes were not recovered for visual verification;
this limitation is distinct from having only an abstract.

The authors describe the five non-reference peptides as randomly chosen **from

peptides or against a selection rule without CaSR. Compound-specific PSE errors
are missing. The quantitative subsection does not establish its own final pH;
do not copy the five-point subsection's range into it.

The HEK assay uses transient hCaSR, Calcium-4/FlexStation and0.9mM CaCl2.
Its pH/NaCl were not recovered explicitly. The separate oocyte protocol uses
different buffer conditions and minimum-effective-concentration measurements;
those are not HEK EC50s.

An apparent internal discrepancy must remain unresolved: Table1 parenthetical
GSH EC50 is0.71uM in the retrieved original text, whereas Figure3A's caption
reports76.5nM. Other table/caption values also differ. Both locators are stored;
neither is silently corrected, averaged or chosen to improve an association.
Original-page verification or a correction is needed to distinguish a printed
discrepancy from text-extraction error. This does not retroactively change the
completed three-compound proportional-scale audit into a broader refutation.

## 2. Broadhead2011: a separate measured receptor panel with uncertainty

The complete original PDF was recovered through an author-posted copy and
visually checked at its assay methods, statistical methods and Table1. These
are actual published measurements, not values copied from a review:

| Peptide | HEK CaR EC50, uM | Reported SE, uM | Experiments, n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| S-methylglutathione |1.7|0.5|4|
| GSH |3.9|0.7|4|
| gamma-Glu-Cys |4.7|0.9|3|
| gamma-Glu-Ala |4.8|0.7|3|

The stable CaR-expressing HEK293 assay uses fura2-AM and baseline-subtracted
integrated fluorescence,2.5mM extracellular calcium, pH7.4 and125mM NaCl.
Human receptor provenance follows its cited Mun2005 protocol, whose indexed
primary Materials identify the human construct. General statistical methods
specify means with SE and numbers of experiments. Individual imaged cells are
not independent experimental n. Table1 lists GSH n4 while Figure1C lists n5;
the table's n remains attached to its EC50, without a guessed reconciliation.

This is a different author/institution group from Ohsu/Amino. It supports
functional receptor evidence in another research setting; it has **no human
saltiness experiment**. Its human parathyroid calcium/PTH measurements remain
separate physiological endpoints. No inference of oral salt enhancement follows
from those measurements.

## 3. Why the receptor feature must identify its assay

For three overlapping named peptides, the published values are:

| Peptide | Ohsu Figure3A, uM | Ohsu Table1 HEK, uM, retrieved text | Broadhead Table1, uM, mean ±SE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GSH |0.0765|0.71|3.9 ±0.7|
| gamma-Glu-Cys |0.458|0.46|4.7 ±0.9|
| gamma-Glu-Ala |3.65|3.7|4.8 ±0.7|

Unit conversion here only renders nM as uM. This is a descriptive comparison,
not a new agreement/rank test or an estimate of a universal assay noise floor.
Calcium, stable/transient expression, readout and response integration co-vary;
the differences cannot be attributed to calcium alone. Neither published SE nor
an apparent within-paper disagreement supplies uncertainty for assay transfer.
Three overlapping peptides do not justify a calibration model.

Ohsu's Emax values are also stored separately from EC50. Partial response
amplitude relative to calcium must not be lost when describing receptor
activity as one potency number. No numerical saturation/exposure extrapolation
to human tasting concentrations was made.

## 4. Amendment to the planned test

`Next_Decision_Test.md` remains the controlling full design: hypothesis,
competing explanation, actual data eligibility, independent unit, comparison,
practical margin, uncertainty and decision consequences.

This assessment adds two concrete gates:

1. Freeze the **assay and response definition** of the CaSR feature before
   examining evaluation outcomes. Preserve EC50, Emax, calcium, pH, expression
   and readout. Mixed protocols require a separately justified transfer method;
   do not choose whichever published number favors the expected result.
2. Require a separately elicited saltiness contrast for the primary human
   endpoint. Retain thickness in NaCl as related evidence; do not assume that
   stimulus identity makes different perceptual rating tasks interchangeable.

The hypothesis stays predictive, not a claim of exclusive CaSR mediation.
Another taste pathway can explain part of a response while CaSR might still
have selection value; this must be evaluated by the planned incremental
comparison, not decided by mechanistic plausibility alone.

### Coverage after this recovery

The existing bounded saltiness/receptor overlap remains five named identities,
four numerical and one graphical-only. Its nine numerical formulation/dose
conditions and eleven endpoint values remain unchanged. The Ohsu six-identity
kokumi panel and Broadhead four-peptide receptor table are separately indexed;
they do not expand the saltiness benchmark. All inspected material remains
development evidence, not independent confirmation.

**Next precise action:** qualify any newly available candidate-level primary
table against the existing design, including the now-explicit assay definition
and saltiness-rating gates. Neither recovered panel qualifies. A newly accessible
original Ohsu page could resolve its internal numerical discrepancy, but would
be documentation work, not the missing saltiness-selection test. Do not launch
another retrieval loop, calibration fit or model sweep around these records.
